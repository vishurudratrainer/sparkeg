### 1. Shuffle Partitions Formula

To avoid small-file overhead or massive out-of-memory errors during wide transformations, calculate the optimal partition count based on your uncompressed data size:

$$\text{spark.sql.shuffle.partitions} = \frac{\text{Total Uncompressed Shuffle Data Size (in MB)}}{\text{Target Partition Size (typically } 128 \text{ MB)}}$$

* **Rule of Thumb:** Aim for **100 MB to 200 MB** per shuffle partition. For instance, if your query moves $50 \text{ GB}$ ($51,200 \text{ MB}$) of shuffle data, the optimal partition count is:

$$\frac{51,200 \text{ MB}}{128 \text{ MB}} = 400 \text{ partitions}$$

### 2. Auto Broadcast Join Threshold Formula

Broadcasting small tables avoids expensive network shuffles by copying the dataset to all executor nodes. However, setting this too high will crash executors with `OutOfMemoryError`.

The safe maximum threshold per executor core can be estimated by:

$$\text{Auto Broadcast Threshold} \le \frac{\text{Executor Heap Memory} \times \text{Safety Fraction}}{\text{Number of Cores per Executor}}$$

* **Rule of Thumb:** A safety fraction of **$0.05$ to $0.10$** (5% to 10% of total executor memory) ensures the broadcast copy doesn't starve execution memory.
* *Example:* For a 16 GB executor, a safe maximum threshold is roughly $16 \text{ GB} \times 0.05 \approx 800 \text{ MB}$ (expressed in bytes as `838860800`).

### 3. Spark Memory Management Formula

Spark divides the JVM heap space into reserved memory, user memory, and unified memory (which splits dynamically between execution and storage).

$$\text{Usable Spark Memory} = \text{spark.memory.fraction} \times (\text{JVM Heap Size} - 300 \text{ MB})$$

Within that unified memory pool:

* **Execution Memory** (used for shuffles, joins, and aggregations):

$$\text{Max Execution Pool} = (1 - \text{spark.memory.storageFraction}) \times \text{Usable Spark Memory}$$


* **Storage Memory** (used for caching and broadcasts):

$$\text{Max Storage Pool} = \text{spark.memory.storageFraction} \times \text{Usable Spark Memory}$$


* *Defaults:* `spark.memory.fraction = 0.6` (60% of heap) and `spark.memory.storageFraction = 0.5` (half of that for storage, half for execution).

---

### 4. Executor Memory Overhead Formula

Non-JVM memory (such as off-heap buffers, Python worker processes for PySpark, and container administrative overhead) requires dedicated buffer space outside the Spark JVM heap to prevent YARN or Kubernetes from killing the container:

$$\text{spark.executor.memoryOverhead} = \max \left( 384 \text{ MB}, \, 0.07 \times \text{spark.executor.memory} \right)$$

* **Rule of Thumb:** If you run heavy PySpark workflows or use window functions/UDFs extensively, scale this up to **$0.15 \times \text{spark.executor.memory}$** or higher.

---

### 5. Cluster Concurrency / Parallelism Formula

To fully saturate your cluster resources without queueing tasks, your total task parallelism should align with your total available core capacity:

$$\text{Total Concurrent Tasks} = \text{Number of Executors} \times \text{Cores per Executor}$$

* **Rule of Thumb:** For optimal pipeline efficiency, ensure your total shuffle partitions or RDD partitions are roughly **$2 \times$ to $3 \times$** your total concurrent task capacity so that cores remain busy processing subsequent task queues instantly.
